## Set P5: Discrete distributions

### Easy

**P5.E1** · MCQ · 1 mark. X ~ Binomial(10, 0.3). E[X] is (A) 2.1 (B) 3 (C) 0.3 (D) 7

**P5.E2** · NAT · 1 mark. Find Var(X) for X ~ Binomial(10, 0.3). (Round off to one decimal place)

**P5.E3** · MCQ · 1 mark. X ~ Poisson(4). Var(X) is (A) 2 (B) 4 (C) 16 (D) 8

**P5.E4** · NAT · 1 mark. X ~ Poisson(2). Find P(X = 0). (Round off to two decimal places)

**P5.E5** · MSQ · 1 mark. X ~ Bernoulli(p) with 0 < p < 1. Which are correct? (A) Var(X) = p(1 − p) (B) Var(X) is largest when p = 0.5 (C) X² has a different distribution from X (D) E[X] = 1 − p

**P5.E6** · NAT · 1 mark. Each trial succeeds with probability 0.25, independently. Find the expected number of trials up to and including the first success.

**P5.E7** · MCQ · 1 mark. X is uniform on {1, 2, …, 10}. E[X] is (A) 5 (B) 5.5 (C) 6 (D) 10

**P5.E8** · MCQ · 1 mark. A fair coin is tossed 4 times. The probability of exactly 2 heads is (A) 0.25 (B) 0.375 (C) 0.5 (D) 0.625

### Medium

**P5.M1** · NAT · 2 marks. X ~ Binomial(5, 0.4). Find P(X ≥ 4). (Round off to three decimal places)

**P5.M2** · MCQ · 2 marks. X ~ Poisson(3). P(X ≤ 1) is closest to (A) 0.05 (B) 0.15 (C) 0.20 (D) 0.42

**P5.M3** · NAT · 2 marks. Calls arrive as a Poisson process at 2 per minute. Find the probability of no calls in a 3-minute interval. (Round off to four decimal places)

**P5.M4** · MSQ · 2 marks. X ~ Poisson(1) and Y ~ Poisson(2) are independent. Which are correct? (A) X − Y ~ Poisson(−1) (B) P(X + Y = 0) = e^(−3) (C) Var(X + Y) = 9 (D) X + Y ~ Poisson(3)

**P5.M5** · NAT · 2 marks. X ~ Binomial(100, 0.02). Using the Poisson approximation, find P(X = 0). (Round off to two decimal places)

**P5.M6** · MCQ · 2 marks. X counts the tosses of a fair coin up to and including the first head. P(X > 5 | X > 2) is (A) 0.03 (B) 0.25 (C) 0.125 (D) 0.5

**P5.M7** · MSQ · 2 marks. A box of 10 bulbs contains 3 defective ones. Four bulbs are chosen without replacement. Which statements are correct? (A) P(none is defective) = 1/6 (B) The expected number of defective bulbs is 1.2 (C) The number of defective bulbs is Binomial(4, 0.3) (D) P(exactly one is defective) = 1/2

**P5.M8** · NAT · 2 marks. X ~ Poisson(3). Find E[X(X − 1)].

**P5.M9** · MCQ · 2 marks. X ~ Binomial(n, p) has mean 6 and variance 4.2. Then (A) n = 30, p = 0.2 (B) n = 10, p = 0.6 (C) n = 20, p = 0.3 (D) n = 15, p = 0.4

### Hard

**P5.H1** · NAT · 2 marks. Find the most likely value (the mode) of X ~ Binomial(10, 0.35).

**P5.H2** · MSQ · 2 marks. X ~ Poisson(λ) with P(X = 1) = P(X = 2). Which statements are correct? (A) λ = 2 (B) Var(X) = 4 (C) P(X = 0) = e^(−2) (D) P(X = 3) ≈ 0.18

**P5.H3** · MCQ · 2 marks. X and Y are independent, each counting the trials up to and including the first success with success probability p. P(X = Y) is (A) p² (B) 1/2 (C) p/(1 − p) (D) p/(2 − p)

**P5.H4** · NAT · 2 marks. A fair coin is tossed until the third head appears. Find the expected number of tosses.

**P5.H5** · MSQ · 2 marks. X ~ Binomial(n, p) with 0 < p < 1. Which are correct? (A) P(X = k + 1)/P(X = k) = (n − k)p/((k + 1)(1 − p)) (B) n − X ~ Binomial(n, p) (C) Var(X) ≥ n/4 (D) If Y ~ Binomial(m, p) is independent of X, then X + Y ~ Binomial(n + m, p)

**P5.H6** · MSQ · 2 marks. A fair die is rolled 6 times. Which statements are correct? (A) P(all six faces appear) = 6!/6⁶ (B) The expected number of faces that never appear is 6(5/6)⁶ (C) The expected number of distinct faces is about 3.99 (D) Face 1 is certain to appear

**P5.H7** · NAT · 2 marks. X ~ Poisson(2). Find E[X | X ≥ 1]. (Round off to two decimal places)

## Set P6: Continuous distributions

### Easy

**P6.E1** · MCQ · 1 mark. X is uniform on (2, 8). Its mean and variance are (A) 5 and 6 (B) 5 and 36 (C) 4 and 3 (D) 5 and 3

**P6.E2** · NAT · 1 mark. X is exponential with mean 4. Find P(X > 4). (Round off to two decimal places)

**P6.E3** · MCQ · 1 mark. Z is standard normal. P(Z > 0) is (A) 0.34 (B) 0 (C) 1 (D) 0.5

**P6.E4** · NAT · 1 mark. X ~ N(10, 4), where 4 is the variance. Find the standardised value z of x = 13. (Round off to one decimal place)

**P6.E5** · MSQ · 1 mark. X is exponential with rate λ. Which are correct? (A) X is memoryless (B) Its median equals its mean (C) Its mean equals its standard deviation (D) It can take negative values

**P6.E6** · MCQ · 1 mark. X is uniform on (0, 10). P(3 < X < 7) is (A) 0.4 (B) 0.3 (C) 0.6 (D) 0.7

**P6.E7** · MCQ · 1 mark. If X ~ N(μ, σ²), then (X − μ)/σ follows (A) N(0, 1) (B) N(0, σ²) (C) t(1) (D) N(μ, 1)

**P6.E8** · NAT · 1 mark. Find the mean of a chi-square distribution with 6 degrees of freedom.

### Medium

**P6.M1** · NAT · 2 marks. X ~ N(50, 25), where 25 is the variance. Find P(45 < X < 60), using Φ(1) = 0.8413 and Φ(2) = 0.9772. (Round off to two decimal places)

**P6.M2** · MCQ · 2 marks. X is exponential with P(X > 2) = 0.25. E[X] is (A) 1/ln 2 (B) ln 2 (C) 2 ln 2 (D) ln 4

**P6.M3** · NAT · 2 marks. X is exponential with P(X > 5) = 0.3. Find P(X > 15 | X > 5). (Round off to two decimal places)

**P6.M4** · MSQ · 2 marks. Z ~ N(0, 1) and T ~ t(3). Which are correct? (A) P(|T| > 2) > P(|Z| > 2) (B) Both distributions are symmetric about 0 (C) The density of T at 0 is larger than that of Z (D) Var(T) = 1

**P6.M5** · NAT · 2 marks. X and Y are independent standard normals. Find P(X + Y > 2), using Φ(1.414) = 0.9214. (Round off to three decimal places)

**P6.M6** · MCQ · 2 marks. X is uniform on (0, 1) and Y = −ln X. Y follows (A) the uniform distribution on (0, 1) (B) the exponential distribution with mean 2 (C) the standard normal distribution (D) the exponential distribution with mean 1

**P6.M7** · MSQ · 2 marks. Two components fail independently after exponential times X and Y with means 2 and 3 years. Which statements are correct? (A) P(the 2-year component fails first) = 0.6 (B) The expected time until both have failed is 5 years (C) min(X, Y) is exponential with rate 5/6 (D) The expected time to the first failure is 1.2 years

**P6.M8** · NAT · 2 marks. Z is standard normal. Find E[Z⁴].

**P6.M9** · MCQ · 2 marks. Z₁ and Z₂ are independent standard normals. P(Z₁² + Z₂² > 2) is closest to (A) 0.135 (B) 0.632 (C) 0.5 (D) 0.368

### Hard

**P6.H1** · NAT · 2 marks. X is exponential with rate ln 5, and Y = ⌊X⌋ (the floor). Then P(Y = k) = q(1 − q)ᵏ for k = 0, 1, 2, …. Find q. (Round off to one decimal place)

**P6.H2** · NAT · 2 marks. Z is standard normal. Find E[|Z|]. (Round off to two decimal places)

**P6.H3** · MCQ · 2 marks. X is uniform on (−1, 1) and Y = X². P(Y ≤ 0.25) is (A) 0.0625 (B) 0.75 (C) 0.5 (D) 0.25

**P6.H4** · NAT · 2 marks. Z is standard normal and Y = 2Z + 3. Find Var(Y²).

**P6.H5** · MSQ · 2 marks. X₁, …, Xₙ (n ≥ 2) are independent N(μ, σ²) variables with sample mean X̄ and sample variance s². Which are correct? (A) (X̄ − μ)/(s/√n) ~ N(0, 1) (B) X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²) (C) (n − 1)s²/σ² ~ χ²(n − 1) (D) X̄ and s² are independent

**P6.H6** · MSQ · 2 marks. M is the largest of three independent uniform (0, 1) variables. Which statements are correct? (A) The expected smallest of the three is 1/4 (B) E[M] = 3/4 (C) Var(M) = 1/12 (D) P(M ≤ t) = t³ for 0 ≤ t ≤ 1

**P6.H7** · MSQ · 2 marks. X is exponential with mean 1. Which statements are correct? (A) E[X | X < 2] = 1 (B) E[X | X > 2] = 3 (C) P(X > 3 | X > 2) = e^(−3) (D) Var(X | X > 2) = 4
