# How to use this guide
%What HR and behavioural rounds test, and how to prepare answers that are true, specific and short

Technical skill gets you to the final rounds; how you talk about yourself, your work and your choices decides many offers. Service companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture) hold an **HR round** and often a **managerial round (MR)**; product companies run **behavioural interviews** in which every answer is expected to be a concrete story. This guide gives you frameworks, a bank of questions, a resume structure and checklists.

The guide is written for you, but it does not invent facts about you. Wherever a real detail is needed (a grade, a number, a project result, a date), it shows a placeholder like **[your CGPA]** or **[result: what improved, by how much]**. Fill these in from your own records once, keep them in a private notes file, and reuse them in every interview. Never claim anything you cannot explain in depth: interviewers probe.

## How to study this book

1. Read Chapters 1–3 and write your own "tell me about yourself" and six STAR stories (about two hours, once).
2. Read Chapter 4 and draft two-line answers to every question in the bank.
3. Prepare your project and research explanations at three lengths (Chapter 5).
4. Rebuild your resume with Chapter 6; ask a senior or a mentor to review it.
5. Before every interview, read Chapter 8's checklist and the company notes you made with Chapter 7.

> TIP: Rehearse **out loud**, ideally recorded on your phone. Answers that read well on paper are often too long when spoken. Aim for 60–90 seconds per answer unless asked for detail.

# Chapter 1: How HR and Behavioural Rounds Work
%What each type of round is for, what interviewers write down, and the red flags they look for

## 1.1 Types of rounds

| Round | Typical length | What it checks | Typical questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| HR (service companies) | 10–20 min | Communication, attitude, fit with company policies (relocation, shifts, service agreement) | Tell me about yourself; why this company; are you willing to relocate? |
| Managerial (MR) | 15–30 min | Maturity, handling pressure and conflict, a deeper look at projects | A conflict in your team; a time you failed; explain your project to a client |
| Behavioural (product companies) | 30–60 min | Specific past behaviour as evidence of future behaviour (ownership, impact, collaboration, learning) | "Tell me about a time when…" questions, each probed with follow-ups |
| Hiring manager | 30–45 min | Motivation, depth on your strongest work, team fit | Your best project in depth; what you want to learn here |

## 1.2 What the interviewer writes down

| They look for | Strong signal | Weak signal |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Answers in a structure, gets to the point | Long, wandering stories |
| Ownership | "I decided…", "I fixed…" with your exact role | "We did…" with no individual contribution |
| Impact | Numbers, outcomes, what changed | Activities without results |
| Learning | What you would do differently, and did next time | Blaming others or circumstances |
| Honesty | Admits gaps, explains how they are closing them | Bluffing, then collapsing under follow-ups |
| Motivation | Specific reasons for this role and company | Generic praise ("great company, good growth") |

## 1.3 Red flags to avoid

| Red flag | Instead |
|---|---|
| Criticising a previous team, teacher or college | Describe the situation neutrally and focus on what you did |
| Memorised, robotic answers | Know your key points, then speak naturally |
| No questions for the interviewer | Prepare two or three real questions (Chapter 7) |
| Inconsistency with the resume | Know every line of your resume; delete what you cannot explain |
| Salary or leave as your first question | Ask about the role and learning first; discuss compensation when they raise it |

## 1.4 Practical lessons

- Each round has a purpose; prepare for the purpose, not just the questions.
- Specific stories with your own role and a result beat general claims.
- Honesty is a scoring criterion, not just a virtue.

# Chapter 2: Tell Me About Yourself
%A 60–90-second answer built from present, past and future

## 2.1 The structure

| Part | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Present | 15–20 s | Who you are now: degree, college, focus |
| Past | 30–45 s | Two or three proof points: a project, research, an achievement, each with one concrete detail |
| Future | 15–20 s | What you want next and why this role fits |

Do not recite your resume from Class 10. Do not start with your family or your hometown unless asked. Pick proof points that match the role: for an SDE role, lead with engineering work; for an AI/ML role, lead with ML work.

We looked at the cost of trying a cheaper option first and escalating when it is not good enough, what we called the **escalation tax**. **[One sentence on your method or finding, in plain words.]**" |

|---|---|
| Service company (TCS, Infosys) | Emphasise learning ability, fundamentals, teamwork and willingness to work across technologies; keep research to one sentence |

| AI/ML role | Lead with the research paper and ML projects; mention evaluation and production thinking (ML System Design guide) |

## 2.3 Practical lessons

- Present, past, future in about 90 seconds.
- Two or three proof points, each with one specific detail.
- Tailor the closing line to the company; never deliver the same answer everywhere.

# Chapter 3: Behavioural Stories with STAR
%Building a story bank once and reusing it for dozens of questions

## 3.1 The STAR structure

| Part | Share of the answer | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | 10–15% | Context in one or two sentences: where, when, what was at stake |
| **T**ask | 10% | Your responsibility or goal |
| **A**ction | 50–60% | What **you** did, step by step, with your reasoning; say "I", not "we" |
| **R**esult | 20% | The outcome with a number if possible, and what you learned |

Product-company interviewers then probe: "Why did you choose that approach?", "What would you do differently?", "What did your teammate think?" A good story survives three follow-ups because it is true and you remember the details.

## 3.2 Build a story bank

Prepare six to eight stories from projects, research, internships, hackathons, college events or part-time work. Each story can answer several questions:

| Story (fill in from your experience) | Answers questions about |
|---|---|
| The research paper: a design decision or a setback during the work | Initiative, dealing with ambiguity, learning, collaboration |
| A project with a hard technical bug you tracked down | Problem solving, persistence, ownership |
| A team project where members disagreed or someone did not deliver | Conflict, teamwork, leadership without authority |
| A deadline you nearly missed | Pressure, prioritisation, communication |
| A mistake you made | Failure, accountability, learning |
| Something you learned quickly for a task | Learning agility, adaptability |
| A time you helped someone else succeed | Mentoring, collaboration |
| Feedback you received and acted on | Coachability, growth |

## 3.3 A worked STAR template

The story below shows the shape; the bracketed parts must be replaced with what really happened.

| Part | What to say |
|---|---|
| Situation | "During our research on choosing between LLMs and reasoning modes, **[describe the point in the project and the problem, e.g. our first experiments gave results we could not explain]**." |
| Task | "I was responsible for **[your part: running the experiments, building the evaluation, writing a section]**, and we had **[deadline or constraint]**." |
| Action | "First I **[step 1, e.g. checked whether the evaluation itself was wrong]**. Then I **[step 2]**, because **[reason]**. I discussed **[decision]** with Suseta, and we agreed to **[decision]**." |
| Result | "**[Outcome with a number or a clear change]**. The main thing I learned was **[lesson]**, and since then I **[how you apply it]**." |

## 3.4 Practical lessons

- Write the stories once, in detail, then practise telling them in 2 minutes.
- Spend most of the time on your actions and your reasoning.
- End with a result and a lesson; prepare for three follow-up questions on each story.
