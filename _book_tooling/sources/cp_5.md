Business-skills questions are mostly **situational**: a short workplace scenario and four plausible responses. The best answer is usually the one that is ethical, customer-focused, proactive, collaborative and escalates appropriately, without blaming anyone. The rest of the questions test named frameworks (the 7 Cs, Tuckman's stages, the Eisenhower matrix, Scrum roles) and basic business arithmetic. This chapter covers both.

## 10.1 Business communication

**The 7 Cs of communication:** clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.

| Situation | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Professional e-mail | Specific subject line; greeting; purpose in the first sentence; short paragraphs or bullets; clear call to action with a date; polite close and signature |
| Reply-all, CC and BCC | Reply-all only when everyone needs the reply; CC for people who should be informed; BCC to hide recipients in large external mailings |
| Bad news or a delay | Inform early, state the impact and the new plan, take ownership, avoid blame |
| Escalation | Facts, impact, what has been tried, what decision or help is needed, by when |
| Instant messages | Complete question in one message ("no hello" etiquette), respect status and working hours |
| Meetings | Agenda shared in advance, start and end on time, minutes with owners and dates for action items |
| Active listening | Attend fully, paraphrase to confirm understanding, ask open questions, do not interrupt |
| Feedback | Specific and timely, about behaviour not personality: the SBI model (Situation, Behaviour, Impact); receive it without defensiveness and say thank you |
| Presentations | Know the audience, one key message, structure (tell them what you will say, say it, summarise), simple visuals, rehearse |

Communication is also non-verbal (tone, eye contact, posture) and the medium matters: complex or sensitive topics deserve a call or a meeting rather than a long e-mail thread.

## 10.2 Professionalism, emotional intelligence and conflict

**Emotional intelligence** (Goleman): self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills.

| Conflict-handling mode (Thomas–Kilmann) | Assertive / cooperative | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Competing | High / low | Emergencies, unpopular but necessary decisions |
| **Collaborating** | High / high | Both sides' concerns matter; time allows a win–win |
| Compromising | Medium / medium | Quick, acceptable solution; equal power |
| Avoiding | Low / low | Trivial issue, or emotions need to cool |
| Accommodating | Low / high | The issue matters more to the other side; preserving the relationship |

Workplace etiquette covers punctuality, appropriate dress and language, respecting diverse cultures, beliefs and time zones, camera and mute discipline in virtual meetings, and keeping commitments or flagging early when you cannot.

## 10.3 Ethics and compliance

| Principle | In practice |
|---|---|
| Integrity | Report facts truthfully, including your own mistakes; never falsify timesheets, data or test results |
| Confidentiality | Client and company information stays inside authorised channels; no client data on personal devices or public AI tools |
| Conflict of interest | Disclose personal relationships or outside interests that could influence decisions |
| Gifts and hospitality | Follow the policy; decline anything that could influence a decision; declare when required |
| Anti-bribery and corruption | Never offer or accept improper payments, directly or through third parties |
| Data privacy and security | Need-to-know access, strong passwords, report phishing and lost devices immediately |
| Respectful workplace | Zero tolerance for harassment (India's POSH Act); equal treatment |
| Whistle-blowing | Report violations through official channels; retaliation is prohibited |
| Intellectual property | Respect licences and copyright; work products belong to the employer or client per contract |

> TIP: In ethics scenarios, eliminate options that ignore the problem, handle it secretly, or break a rule "just this once". The best answer usually involves raising it with the manager or the proper channel, promptly and factually.

## 10.4 Teamwork, leadership and motivation

**Tuckman's stages of team development:** forming (polite, unclear roles) → storming (conflict over roles and approaches) → norming (agreed ways of working) → performing (high productivity) → adjourning (closure).

| Leadership style | Description | Works when |
|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | Leader decides alone | Crises, inexperienced teams |
| Democratic (participative) | Team input before decisions | Creative work, buy-in needed |
| Laissez-faire | Minimal direction | Expert, self-motivated teams |
| Transformational | Inspires with vision, develops people | Change and innovation |
| Servant | Leader serves the team's needs, removes obstacles | Agile teams (the Scrum Master role) |
| Situational | Adapts style to the team's competence and commitment | Most real situations |

| Motivation theory | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Maslow's hierarchy | Physiological → safety → belonging → esteem → self-actualisation; lower needs first |
| Herzberg's two-factor | **Hygiene factors** (salary, policies, conditions) prevent dissatisfaction; **motivators** (achievement, recognition, growth, responsibility) create satisfaction |
| McGregor's Theory X / Y | X: people dislike work and need control; Y: people seek responsibility and self-direct |

Delegation: match tasks to skills and development goals, explain the outcome and the deadline, give authority along with responsibility, and check in without micromanaging.

## 10.5 Problem solving and decision making

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| 5 Whys | Ask "why?" repeatedly to move from symptom to root cause |
| Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram | Group possible causes: people, process, technology, materials, environment, measurement |
| Pareto principle (80/20) | Roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes: fix the vital few first |
| SWOT | Strengths and weaknesses (internal), opportunities and threats (external) |
| PESTLE | Political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental factors |
| Porter's five forces | Rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, buyer power, supplier power |
| Decision matrix | Score options against weighted criteria |
| Design thinking | Empathise → define → ideate → prototype → test |
| Critical thinking | Separate facts from assumptions, check sources, consider alternatives and biases (confirmation bias, anchoring) |

## 10.6 Time management and goals

| Tool | Idea |
|---|---|
| Eisenhower matrix | Urgent and important: do now; important, not urgent: schedule; urgent, not important: delegate; neither: eliminate |
| SMART goals | Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound |
| MoSCoW prioritisation | Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have (this time) |
| Pomodoro technique | 25 minutes of focused work, 5-minute break |
| Single-tasking | Context switching costs time; batch similar tasks |
| Managing up | Agree priorities with your manager when demands conflict; say early if a deadline is at risk |

## 10.7 Customers and stakeholders

| Topic | Key points |
|---|---|
| Customer-centricity | Understand the customer's goals, not just the request; under-promise, over-deliver |
| Handling complaints (LAST) | Listen, Apologise, Solve, Thank; stay calm, never argue or blame, follow up |
| Service metrics | NPS (likelihood to recommend, −100 to +100), CSAT (satisfaction score), CES (effort), SLA (agreed service levels such as response and resolution times) |
| Stakeholder management | Power–interest grid: manage closely (high, high), keep satisfied (high power), keep informed (high interest), monitor (low, low) |
| Saying no | Explain constraints, offer alternatives or a later date, escalate scope changes through change control |

## 10.8 Projects and Agile delivery

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Triple constraint | Scope, time and cost (quality in the middle): changing one affects the others |
| Waterfall | Sequential phases (requirements → design → build → test → deploy); suits fixed, well-understood requirements |
| Agile | Iterative, incremental delivery with customer feedback (Agile Manifesto: individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, responding to change) |
| Scrum roles | **Product Owner** (owns and prioritises the product backlog, maximises value), **Scrum Master** (servant leader, coaches the process, removes impediments), **Developers** (build the increment, self-managing) |
| Scrum events | Sprint (1–4 weeks), sprint planning, daily scrum (15 minutes), sprint review (demo to stakeholders), sprint retrospective (improve the process) |
| Scrum artefacts | Product backlog, sprint backlog, increment; definition of done |
| User stories | "As a <user>, I want <goal> so that <benefit>"; good stories are INVEST: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable |
| Estimation | Story points, planning poker; velocity = points completed per sprint |
| Kanban | Visual board, limits on work in progress (WIP), continuous flow |
| RACI matrix | Responsible, Accountable (one person), Consulted, Informed |
| Risk management | Identify, assess (probability × impact), respond (avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept), monitor |
| Critical path | Longest chain of dependent tasks; any delay on it delays the project |

## 10.9 Business numbers

```python run Margins, break-even, ROI and CAGR for a small IT services deal
revenue, direct_costs, overheads = 50_00_000, 30_00_000, 12_00_000         # rupees per year
gross_profit = revenue - direct_costs
net_profit = gross_profit - overheads
print(f'gross margin {gross_profit / revenue:.0%}   net margin {net_profit / revenue:.0%}')

price, variable_cost, fixed_costs = 1200, 700, 6_00_000                    # per licence
contribution = price - variable_cost
print(f'break-even: {fixed_costs / contribution:,.0f} licences '
      f'(contribution {contribution} per licence)')

investment, gain = 8_00_000, 11_00_000
print(f'ROI: {(gain - investment) / investment:.1%}')

start, end, years = 120, 210, 4                      # revenue in crores
print(f'CAGR: {(end / start) ** (1 / years) - 1:.1%} per year')
```

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue / turnover | Money earned from sales |
| Gross profit, gross margin | Revenue − direct costs; as a share of revenue |
| Net profit, net margin | After all expenses, interest and tax |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (operating performance) |
| Break-even point | Fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit) |
| ROI | (Gain − investment) ÷ investment |
| CAGR | (End ÷ start)^(1/years) − 1 |
| Capex vs opex | Long-term asset spending (servers) versus running costs (cloud subscription) |
| B2B, B2C, B2B2C | Selling to businesses, consumers, or consumers through businesses |
| Business models | Subscription (SaaS), freemium, marketplace, pay-per-use, licensing, advertising |
| TAM, SAM, SOM | Total, serviceable and obtainable market |
| KPIs | Measurable indicators of success (revenue growth, churn, utilisation, NPS) |
| IT services terms | Onsite/offshore delivery, fixed price versus time and materials (T&M) contracts, SLA, utilisation, attrition |

## 10.10 How to answer situational questions

1. Identify the core issue (ethics, customer, deadline, conflict, quality).
2. Eliminate answers that ignore it, blame others, break rules, or escalate everything without trying.
3. Prefer answers that act promptly, communicate transparently, involve the right people and protect the customer and the company.
4. Between two good answers, pick the one that addresses the **root cause** rather than the symptom.


# Chapter 11: Exam Strategy
%Time planning, MCQ technique, the coding-round routine, and the traps most worth remembering

## 11.1 Time plan

The duration is shown on the admit card or instructions; divide it by marks. For example, for a 120-minute paper:

| Section | Marks | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|
| Python coding | 35 | 45 minutes (including 10 minutes of testing with your own inputs) |
| Technical quiz (40 questions) | 40 | 45 minutes (about 1 minute each; code questions take longer, theory less) |
| Business skills (25 questions) | 25 | 20 minutes |
| Review | | 10 minutes |

Check whether sections are separately timed and whether you can move between them; read the instructions on negative marking before answering questions you are unsure about.

## 11.2 MCQ technique

- Read the question stem **before** the code; know what you are asked to predict.
- For code questions, trace on paper: write down variables after each line; watch indentation, integer division, mutability and in-place methods returning `None`.
- Eliminate options that are absolutes ("always", "never") unless you know they are true.
- Map scenario keywords to concepts: "no labels" → unsupervised; "rewards" → reinforcement; "rare positive class" → precision/recall/F1; "answers must cite internal documents" → RAG; "needs to call APIs and decide steps" → agent; "change tone or format" → fine-tuning or prompting.
- Mark uncertain questions and return; never leave the coding section for the end.

## 11.3 The coding routine

1. Read twice; list classes, attributes (with types and order), methods (names, parameters, returns) and output messages.
2. Write the classes; then main reading input exactly as described.
3. Run the sample; then test a "no match" input and a tie.
4. Re-read the output format character by character (spaces, case, decimals with `round` or formatting).

## 11.4 Forty traps worth remembering

| Topic | Trap |
|---|---|
| Python | `list.sort()` returns `None`; `sorted()` returns a list |
| Python | Mutable default argument `def f(x=[])` is shared across calls |
| Python | `a = b` for lists copies the reference; use `b.copy()` or `b[:]` |
| Python | `print` evaluates all its arguments before printing |
| Python | Instance attribute assignment hides a class attribute of the same name |
| Python | `input()` returns a string; convert before comparing numbers |
| Python | `__x` attributes are name-mangled to `_Class__x` |
| Python | `max(key=...)` returns the first maximum on ties |
| SQL | `= NULL` never matches; use `IS NULL` |
| SQL | `COUNT(*)` counts rows, `COUNT(col)` skips NULLs; AVG ignores NULLs |
| SQL | WHERE cannot use aggregates; HAVING filters groups |
| SQL | LEFT JOIN + `COUNT(*)` counts unmatched rows as 1 |
| SQL | `NOT IN` with a NULL in the subquery returns nothing |
| SQL | RANK leaves gaps after ties; DENSE_RANK does not |
| SQL | TRUNCATE is DDL; DELETE is DML and can have WHERE |
| AI basics | Supervised = labels, unsupervised = no labels, reinforcement = rewards |
| AI basics | A* is optimal only with an admissible heuristic |
| AI basics | Self-supervised learning creates labels from the data (LLM pre-training) |
| EDA | `loc` includes the end label; `iloc` excludes it |
| EDA | `value_counts` and `describe` exclude NaN; `describe` skips text columns by default |
| EDA | Median, not mean, for skewed data imputation |
| EDA | Correlation is linear association, not causation |
| EDA | Fit scalers on training data only |
| Supervised | Accuracy misleads on imbalanced data |
| Supervised | Precision: of predicted positives, how many are right; recall: of actual positives, how many are found |
| Supervised | L1 (Lasso) can zero coefficients; L2 (Ridge) only shrinks them |
| Supervised | Training ≫ test score means overfitting |
| Unsupervised | k-means needs scaling and k; inertia always falls with k |
| Unsupervised | DBSCAN labels noise as −1 and needs no k |
| Unsupervised | PCA needs standardised inputs; components are uncorrelated |
| Unsupervised | Lift > 1 means positive association |
| Advanced ML | Bagging reduces variance (parallel); boosting reduces bias (sequential) |
| Advanced ML | Use the test set once, after tuning |
| Advanced ML | Resample (SMOTE) the training data only |
| Advanced ML | Softmax for multi-class outputs, sigmoid for binary |
| GenAI | Lower temperature = more deterministic; top-p keeps a probability mass |
| GenAI | BERT is encoder-only (understanding); GPT is decoder-only (generation) |
| GenAI | Knowledge → RAG; behaviour/style → fine-tuning; LoRA trains small adapters |
| RAG | Same embedding model for documents and queries; chunk with overlap |
| Agents | The application executes tools; the LLM only proposes calls; add step limits and approvals |
